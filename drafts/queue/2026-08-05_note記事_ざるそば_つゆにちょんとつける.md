## 本文
```
ずずっ、と、いい音を立てて、そばをすする。冷たいそばを、ひと口分、箸で持ち上げて、つゆに、ちょんとつけて、勢いよく、口へ手繰り込む。のどを、つるつると通っていく、この感じ。暑い日に、これほど、するっと食べられるものは、そうない。

そばは、あまり、かまない。かむより、のどで味わう、なんてことも言われる。ほんとうかどうかは、わからないけれど、たしかに、あの、するするとのどを通る感触は、そばの、大事な楽しみの一つだ。夏の昼に、冷たいざるそばが出てくると、それだけで、少し、ほっとする。

[写真①]

■ つゆは、少しだけ

そばのつゆのつけ方には、なんとなく、流儀のようなものがある。そば全体を、どっぷりつゆにひたすのではなく、先のほうを、ちょんと、少しだけつける。「粋な食べ方は、つゆを少しだけ」と、よく聞く。

もっとも、これには、諸説ある。たっぷりつけたほうがうまい、という人もいる。結局は、好みだ。私は、というと、気分による。上等なそばのときは、香りを味わおうと、少しだけ。おなかがすいているときは、がっつり、たっぷりつける。粋も何も、あったものではない。

■ 薬味の、いい仕事

ざるそばには、薬味がついてくる。刻んだねぎと、わさび。これを、つゆに溶く。ねぎの、しゃきっとした辛み。わさびの、つんと鼻に抜ける刺激。この二つが入るだけで、つゆの味が、ぐっと引き締まる。

わさびを、どのくらい入れるか。これも、悩ましい。効かせすぎると、鼻の奥が、つーんと痛くなって、涙が出る。でも、あの、痛いような刺激が、また、くせになる。ときどき、量をまちがえて、悶絶しながら、それでも、また入れてしまう。学習しない。

[写真②]

■ 最後の、そば湯

ざるそばの、隠れた楽しみが、最後の、そば湯だ。そばをゆでた、あの、白く濁ったゆで汁。これを、残ったつゆに注いで、飲む。そばの風味が溶けたつゆが、あたたかい湯で、ちょうどいい濃さに薄まって、じんわりと、うまい。

そばを食べ終わって、さて、と、そば湯を注ぐ。この、シメの一杯があると、なんだか、食事が、きちんと終わった気がする。冷たいそばで、するっと始まって、あたたかいそば湯で、ほっと終わる。この流れが、なんというか、なかなか、いいのだ。

【English】Zaru soba — just a little dip in the tsujiru.

With a good slurp — zzzt — you pull the soba in. Lift a mouthful of the cold noodles with your chopsticks, dip just the tips into the dipping sauce, and haul them briskly into your mouth, and they slide down your throat. On a hot day, few things go down this smoothly. You don't chew soba much — "taste it with your throat, not your teeth," they even say; whether that's true I don't know, but that slide down the throat really is one of soba's chief pleasures, and a cold zaru soba at a summer noon settles you a little just by arriving.

There's a sort of etiquette to the dip: not soaking the whole strand, but touching just the tip lightly into the sauce — "the stylish way is only a little sauce," you often hear. Though there are other views — some say plenty is better — so in the end it's taste. Me, it depends on the mood: with good soba I dip a little, to catch the aroma; hungry, I soak it deep, and never mind stylish. The garnish does fine work: chopped scallion and wasabi, stirred into the sauce — the scallion's crisp bite, the wasabi's sharp rush up the nose — and with just those two the sauce tightens right up. How much wasabi is the tricky part: overdo it and the back of the nose stings till your eyes water — and yet that near-painful jolt is habit-forming, so now and then I misjudge, writhe, and put it in again anyway. I don't learn.

Zaru soba's hidden pleasure is the last of it: soba-yu, the cloudy white water the noodles were boiled in. Pour it into the leftover sauce and drink — the sauce, steeped with soba's flavor, thinned by the warm water to just the right strength, is quietly good. Noodles finished, you pour the soba-yu, and with that closing cup the meal feels properly ended: a smooth cold start, a warm, settling finish. That flow, well — it's pretty good. (The dip, the garnish and how you take soba-yu vary by region, person and view; this is our one example. Those with a soba allergy, take care, and mind food safety.)

## 英語要約
```
With a good slurp you pull the cold soba in — lift a mouthful, dip just the tips into the sauce, haul it into your mouth, and it slides down your throat; on a hot day few things go down this smoothly. You don't chew soba much ("taste it with your throat," they say — true or not, that slide is a chief pleasure), and a cold zaru soba at a summer noon settles you just by arriving. There's an etiquette: touch only the tip into the sauce ("the stylish way is a little"), though some say plenty is better, so it's taste — for a Takaoka resident it depends on mood: good soba, a little dip for the aroma; hungry, soaked deep, never mind stylish. The garnish — chopped scallion and wasabi stirred in — tightens the sauce; how much wasabi is tricky, since overdoing it stings the nose till the eyes water, yet that near-painful jolt is habit-forming, so he misjudges, writhes, and does it again — doesn't learn. The hidden pleasure is last: soba-yu, the cloudy boiling water, poured into the leftover sauce and drunk, thinned to just the right strength, quietly good — a cold start, a warm finish, and the meal feels properly ended. That flow is pretty good. (Dip, garnish and soba-yu vary by region and taste; mind soba allergy and food safety.)
