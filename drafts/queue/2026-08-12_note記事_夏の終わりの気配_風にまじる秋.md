夏の終わりは、いつも、こんなふうに、風から、やってくる。夏が、真っ盛りの、あいだは、気づかない。でも、お盆を、過ぎると、ある日、ふと、風の質が、変わる。あの、気づいたときの、なんとも言えない、気持ち。うれしいような、さびしいような。夏が、ゆっくりと、終わりに、向かいはじめている。

【English】A sign of summer's end — autumn mixing into the wind.

Around when Obon has passed. The daytime is still blazing hot, the sun as strong as ever. But in the evening, at a wind that suddenly blows up, you think, huh? — a touch different from the lukewarm wind of a moment ago; something dry and cool is mixed into that wind. It's still summer. Yet in that wind, ever so faintly, a sign of autumn has begun to mix. Summer's end always comes like this, from the wind. While summer is at its height you don't notice; but once Obon passes, one day the quality of the wind suddenly changes, and the feeling at that noticing is beyond words — glad, and lonely — summer slowly beginning to turn toward its end.

It isn't only the wind: the length of the day, too, changes little by little. Around the solstice it was still light past seven, but once Obon passes, the evening darkens a little earlier — huh, dark already, you think, looking at the dusk sky. That endlessly bright summer evening slowly shortens, and into the dusk light, too, a certain clear autumn quality begins to mix; the daytime is still summer, but the evening alone goes a little ahead into autumn — a kind of seasonal lag within a single day is there at summer's end. The night's sounds change too: the cicadas, so loud in the day at the height of summer, somehow lose their vigor once Obon passes, and instead, at night, other insects begin to sing, riin, riin, a clear sound; when that begins, ah, you think.

From the day's stuffy cicada voice to the night's clear insect voice — this baton-pass of sound is a signal of summer's end; opening the window and listening to those night insects, you grow a little pensive — the summer you thought so long is already nearing its close. Summer is ending, and that's a little lonely, though you were sick of the heat; as the end nears, somehow you cling to it, thinking you should have done this and that, remembering the passed days — a feeling like the end of summer vacation. But the seasons turn: summer ends and autumn comes, and that, too, is good; grieving the passing summer a little, you greet the next season. That you can feel this shifting may be one good thing about Japan's four seasons; feeling the faint autumn that has begun to mix into the wind, you start, about now, to prepare to see this summer off too. (How the season's shift appears and feels varies greatly by year, region and place; this is our one example and impression, keeping the meteorology and creatures general. Even with autumn's signs, the lingering heat can be severe — keep guarding against heatstroke.)
