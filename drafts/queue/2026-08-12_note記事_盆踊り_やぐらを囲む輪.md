【English】Bon-odori — the ring around the tower.

In the middle of the square a yagura (tower) is built, a drum booming don, don from its top, and red lanterns lit all around it, faintly lighting the place. Around that tower, people form a ring and dance the same steps slowly. Summer's night bon-odori: seeing that drum's sound and the lantern light, ah, it's summer, I think, deeply. Bon-odori has little to do with being good or bad at it — you step into the ring, copy the person ahead, hands up, hands down, and turn, and after a few rounds the steps somehow enter the body; it's no hard dance, anyone can join the ring, and that openness is its good.

Honestly, I don't properly remember the steps — I dance every year, yet by the next year I've forgotten again, so in the ring I'm always desperately copying the person ahead, half a beat late, hurriedly raising my hands, subtly out of sync with everyone. But that's fine: bon-odori's aim isn't to dance well; it's for everyone to form a ring, make the same movements, and share the air of the place — that, I think, is what matters, so out of sync or mistaken, no one minds, and you just move your body slowly in the ring, that pleasantness. That feel of endlessly repeating the same steps has its own peculiar comfort: riding the drum's monotone rhythm, the same movement over and over, until your head gradually empties and you stop thinking of extra things.

Forming a ring and repeating the same movement together, somehow the border between yourself and those around you grows a little vague — you're not dancing alone, you're part of the ring, inside a larger flow, that sense of oneness, a strange time, lively yet somehow quiet. Bon-odori is usually held around the Obon season, so when it starts, ah, Obon already, I think, and feel summer has entered its later half — that drum's sound is a kind of signal announcing summer's second half. The ring of people around the tower, the drum, the lantern light, the yukata-clad figures, all become one in that summer night's scene; not a flashy event, but the local people gather and dance slowly in the same ring, and that warm time is one of every summer's pleasures. (The origin, dance and songs of bon-odori vary greatly by region and group, and are debated; this is our one example. As it's held at night amid crowds and heat, mind heatstroke, falls and getting lost.)
