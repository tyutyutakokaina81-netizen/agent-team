【English】Making cola over a fire — craft cola simmered from spices on a shichirin.

I always thought cola was something you buy — pop the cap on a cold one and drink. But it turns out you can make it at home: simmer spices, dissolve sugar, cut it with soda, and you get cola. It surprised me a little when I learned that. It started out of curiosity: set a mestin (an aluminum box-shaped mess kit) on the garden shichirin (charcoal brazier), put in spices, sugar and water, and let it simmer, a wonderful smell rising with the steam.

The method is simple: put various spices in water, add sugar, and simmer — that's all. We use cardamom, cinnamon, clove, plus ginger and citrus peel; the amounts are pretty rough, by mood (blends differ completely person to person). Over the heat the spice fragrance rises at once — cardamom's fresh scent, cinnamon's sweet one — and done outside on the shichirin rather than in the kitchen, the charcoal smell mixes in, like a fun little experiment, the spices swaying in the browning liquid.

The charcoal's heat is gentle, so you don't rush — simmer slow, stirring now and then with a twig or chopsticks. It isn't a hurried dish; you sit in a folding chair, watch the fire, and wait for it to cook, and that waiting is good. Making something over a fire outdoors — just that, yet somehow it slows your mind: the smell of spices, the crackle of charcoal, the rising steam. So much trouble for one drink; by efficiency, buying is far faster — but the trouble is the fun.

Once well simmered, take it off the fire and strain; removing the spices leaves a dark brown syrup. Pour a little into a glass of ice, cut it with cold soda — fizz rising — and a squeeze of citrus, and that's our cola. One sip and the spice fragrance spreads; it's a different taste from store cola — the sweetness and the spice are our own, not a dead ringer for that familiar flavor, but good in its own way, with a little satisfaction of having simmered it yourself added to the taste. You could just buy cola, true — but now and then you make it from spices over a fire; some days go well, some come out too sweet or too spicy, and that's part of the fun. Going to the trouble of making one drink over a fire outdoors is just right for a summer break — a home-made pleasure of Japan, and I'd be glad if it came through a little. (Blends, sugar, simmering and mixing have no fixed rule and vary greatly by home and taste; this is our one example, making no health claims. Take great care with fire, charcoal and hot syrup to avoid burns, and mind any allergies to spices or ingredients.)
