■ 皮つきのまま、湯気ごとごちそう

茹で方も、家それぞれだ。うちは、外側の皮を一枚だけ残して、たっぷりの湯でゆでる。皮を全部むかないのは、そのほうが甘い蒸気が逃げにくい、と教わったからだ。ゆで上がって、熱いのを我慢して皮をむくと、黄色い粒がぎっしり並んで、湯気を立てている。この時点でもう、じゅうぶんごちそうだ。

熱いうちに、はしっこからかぶりつく。粒がぷちぷちとはじけて、甘い汁が口に広がる。塩をふってもいいし、何もつけなくてもいい。もぎたてなら、それだけで十分に甘い。歯にはさまるのはご愛敬。夏の食卓の、素朴な楽しみだ。

【English】People who keep a field often say corn is a race against time.

From the moment you pick it, the sweetness starts to fade — so the best thing is to eat it the same day, boiled as soon after picking as you can. Buy an ear at a roadside stand where they tell you "picked this morning," and by the time you get home you're already restless to get it boiling. Boiling styles differ by household. We leave just one layer of husk on and boil it in plenty of water — not peeling it fully, because I was taught the sweet steam escapes less that way. When it's done, you brave the heat to peel it and the yellow kernels stand packed in rows, steaming; at that point it's already a feast. Bite in from the end while it's hot, the kernels bursting with a little pop, sweet juice spreading; salt it or don't — if it's freshly picked it's plenty sweet on its own. Kernels stuck in your teeth are part of the deal — a plain pleasure of the summer table. Different from boiling is the grilled corn you eat at a summer festival or fair, which is something else again: brushed with soy sauce and grilled over charcoal, that toasty smell drifts over everything. On a festival night the smell lures you in and you buy one without meaning to; the sweetness and the toasted soy become the very memory of summer. At home you can get close to that stall taste by brushing boiled corn with a little soy and lightly charring it in a pan or under a grill — mind the burns, since it uses fire, but that toastiness is a summer-only thing. The good thing about corn is that it's sweet on its own, no special cooking needed — but that sweetness won't wait, which is exactly why you eat it fresh in a hurry. The act of hurrying itself feels like summer. Not something to leave in the fridge for days, but taken the same day it's picked, steam and all — a food with nothing to do with efficiency or keeping. I'd be glad if visitors from abroad could know this "chasing freshness" pleasure of a Japanese summer field. Sweetness won't wait — so summer is busy, and fun. (Just my own way; sweetness, freshness and boiling vary by variety and season, and please take care with fire, boiling water and burns.)
