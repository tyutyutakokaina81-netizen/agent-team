【English】Ramune — the marble rattles, koron.

Tip the bottle to drink and inside a marble rattles, koron. A round glass marble sits in the mouth of the glass bottle in place of a cap — that distinctive, pinched-waist bottle — and every time you drink, the marble rolls to the mouth and half-blocks the way, half-doesn't; that slightly hard-to-drink feeling is ramune's peculiar charm. The lights of a festival stall; ramune bottles bobbing in ice water — recall that scene and the taste itself comes back to the mouth: cold, sweet, fizzy. Not an especially fine drink; but see that bottle in summer and your hand reaches out.

To drink it, first the ritual: set the plastic pusher on the bottle's mouth and shove it down hard with your palm; with a pop the marble drops into the bottle, foam surging up, and you hurry your mouth to it — the tension of that first push. Push it badly and the foam sprays out and your hand goes sticky. As a kid I wanted so badly to do the pushing; my siblings and I fought over who got to. Nothing to it, really — just pushing a marble — looking back. Finished drinking, the next thing to rise in your mind is that marble in the emptied bottle: you want it, badly, a clean clear glass ball, and I remember shaking the bottle every which way to get it out. But it doesn't come easily — the bottle's mouth is made smaller than the marble, of course, or the marble would come out while you drank; still, unable to give up, you rock it, turn it over. In the end it never came out — and that so-close-yet-not frustration is, now, a fond memory.

Honestly, ramune's taste itself is plain — not very sweet, not especially delicious. But you're probably not drinking only the taste: the bottle's shape, the marble's sound, the pushing ritual, the stall's lights — you drink all the surroundings together. So on a hot day, see that pinched bottle and, before the taste, summer itself comes back. That koron of the marble — that alone is summer enough. (Ramune's making, the marble mechanism and history vary and are debated; this is our one example. Mind the contents spraying on opening, and the marble as a choking hazard, especially for small children; take care with empty bottles too.)
