Nobody Gives You the Signal

Putting the heater away is easy — it stopped being cold, so it goes back. Getting it out is the harder decision. There are days when the morning is cold and by noon you do not need it, and bringing the thing out for that one morning feels like an overreaction, so you wait. The next day is mild and you are glad you did not bother. The day after that it turns again. Repeat this a few times and at some point it is simply out; ask me which day I decided and I cannot tell you. Part of it is the effort — carrying it from the shed, buying kerosene, the faint burnt smell the first time it is lit — so the judgement leans towards *not yet*. There is a stretch of about two weeks each year where wearing another layer covers it, and I spend that stretch every year. The fuel decides with it: you buy kerosene once you have decided to use it, so the day you get the heater out and the day you buy are linked, and by the time the temperature says *soon* it is usually already cold. Buying ahead would solve that, except that unused kerosene come spring is its own problem. Winter here is long, but the entrance to it drags. Through October I am still undecided; once November starts, I am not. There is one day when it turns sharply cold, and after that nobody says *not yet* any more. While I fail to decide, the weather decides for me. I think I spend every autumn waiting for that.

その一日だけのために出すのは大げさな気がして、もう少し待つことにする。次の日は暖かくて、やっぱり出さなくてよかったと思う。その次の日にまた冷える。

## 出すまでが面倒

物置から運んで、灯油を買ってきて、最初に点けるときは少し煙くさい。
