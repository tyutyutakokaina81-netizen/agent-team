【English】Today I left the company I'd worked at for a long time. Early retirement.

It may sound like a grand decision, but when the day actually came, it was almost anticlimactic — an ordinary morning like any other. I woke at the usual time, got ready the usual way. Only the familiar streets I walked, thinking "this is the last time," looked a little different. Honestly, I wavered for a long time before deciding to leave. Losing a steady paycheck. My family. Being told it was "such a waste." From the outside I probably wasn't at the age, or with the reasons, to quit yet. There was no dramatic event that pushed me, and no one forced me out. It was just that a wish — to spend the time I have left in a shape I'd chosen myself — slowly stopped going away. More than the fear, the thought of "what if I change nothing and stay like this" grew heavier. Let me be clear: I'm not leaving because I came to hate the company. They employed me for years and taught me a great deal, and I feel nothing but gratitude toward the people I worked with. So this isn't an escape or a breakup — just a punctuation mark, quietly bowing my head to a place that looked after me and stepping out. "Early retirement" can sound valiant, but inside I'm far more plain about it: from tomorrow there's no guaranteed income, and no job title on a business card. It would be a lie to say I'm not scared. If anything, I am scared — but I wanted to take on that fear, and all, myself. From here, living in Takaoka, I plan to start small, by my own hand. I can't do anything big at once, so I'll do one thing a day within what I can manage: writing, keeping my own small life in order, making the person in front of me glad. I believe the next version of myself lies at the end of that plain accumulation. I don't want to urge anyone to quit their job — leaving isn't noble, and staying isn't noble either. I only want to tell anyone standing at the same crossroads, worrying in secret late at night, that "there's someone here who stepped forward while still afraid." Today, one chapter closed. Tomorrow, the usual morning comes again — only the direction I walk changes a little from today. And that is both frightening and glad. (This is my own case and feelings; the systems, conditions and financial impact of early retirement differ greatly by person, and this is neither general advice nor a recommendation to quit.)
