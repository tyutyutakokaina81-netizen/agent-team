【English】The summer pool — the smell of chlorine and purple lips.

Near the pool, first that smell hits: the sharp scent of chlorine. The instant it reaches the back of your nose a switch flips in your body — ah, the pool. As a kid that smell alone was almost too much joy; I remember summer days running there, swinging the bag with my swimsuit in it. You dip a toe in — cold — then plunge in with resolve, cold water wrapping your whole body, that moment of release all at once from the heat; however many times, it's good. Pool water may have been the finest feast of a hot summer.

A child, once in, doesn't get out — diving, swimming, splashing, and time flies before you know it, and somewhere along the way your lips have turned purple and your body is shivering; still you hold out, "I don't want to get out yet." A parent or a lifeguard, exasperated — "your lips are purple" — says get out and warm up. Grudgingly out, wrapped in a towel, basking in the sun, and a little warmed you go stubbornly back to the water; a child's day at the pool was this repeated. There was that rest-break signal, too: a whistle, peee, and all at once everyone climbs out, the water that was so lively going dead quiet — that enforced rest, maddening to a child. But looking back it must have been important: stay in the whole time and the body chills too much; over the break you ate shaved ice or spaced out in the sun, and it was that breath that let you go back full of energy. That safety whistle is one of the pool's summer memories too.

Played out, worn to nothing, you head home from the pool, body heavy with soaked water, hair steeped in the chlorine smell, eyes a little bleary — yet the heart is full, that pleasant tiredness of having played to the last. On the way home you get a little sleepy, and though you romped so much, you already can't wait for the next pool day. That summer smell of chlorine, those purple lips, the feel of the cold water — even grown, catch the pool's smell and that summer day comes back, right there. (Water accidents can be fatal: at pools and waterside, keep to supervision, company and the rules, and above all don't push it, according to your condition and the day. This is our one example and memory, and impressions vary by person.)
