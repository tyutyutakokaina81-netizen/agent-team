【English】Tempura — eaten standing, straight from the oil.

The oil sizzles. Lower a battered piece gently into it and fine bubbles rise all at once, then, after a while, gradually quiet — the sign it's done. You scoop it up with a mesh ladle, and the golden, crisp tempura crackles, flicking off oil. Tempura is all about being fresh from the oil: as time passes that vital crispness is lost more and more, so in our house we eat it as it's fried — standing right beside whoever's frying, in the kitchen, huffing and picking at it. Bad manners; but that just-fried piece is the best of it.

What to eat it with is a real dilemma. Dip it in tentsuyu with grated daikon, soaked and dripping — that's good too. But good tempura fresh from the oil is enough with just a sprinkle of salt; rather, that way you taste the ingredient better. We set out both — shrimp and kisu (whiting) with salt, eggplant and kabocha with tentsuyu, using each by the piece — though halfway through, often, it gets to be a bother and everything goes plunging into the tentsuyu. The good of tempura is you can fry anything: in summer, eggplant, kabocha, shishito, myoga, shiso — the summer vegetables from the field or the shopfront, battered one after another, and for seafood, shrimp or kisu; near the sea, the day's fish fried is fine too. I love shiso tempura — that thin leaf, lightly battered and fried, and biting in, shiso's clean fragrance spreads in the mouth; plain, but it does fine work, one coat of batter turning a summer vegetable into a feast.

Honestly, frying tempura at home is a fair bother: preparing the oil, judging the batter, the spitting oil while you fry, the cleanup — the kitchen goes sticky with oil, so we don't do it that often. But now and then, eating it just-fried, all that bother is repaid: crisp, light, hot, the ingredient's taste sealed tight inside; that just-fried bite is unlike any tempura eaten out. "Worth doing, bother and all," I think every time — and then don't do it again for a while. (Batter, ingredients and how you dip vary by taste and view; this is our one example. With deep-frying take great care with flame, hot oil and spitting oil — mind burns and fire — and mind cooking and food safety.)
