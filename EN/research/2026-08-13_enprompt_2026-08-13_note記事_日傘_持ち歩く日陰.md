【English】A parasol — carrying shade with you.

A blazing midsummer road. You snap open a parasol and hold it overhead, and just around your head a patch of shade forms; the sunlight that was searing your head and neck is cut off by that shade. It's only that, yet the felt heat is quite different — a parasol is, so to speak, your own portable shade. Honestly, until a while ago I thought a parasol had little to do with me. But summer's heat has grown harsher year by year, and needs must; I tried holding one, and it was cooler than expected — or rather, it eases the very worst of the heat — and now I can't do without it on a summer outing.

For shade, a straw hat is fine too — for yard work a hat is handier, both hands free. But a parasol has its own good: where a hat guards the head, a parasol covers head, neck and shoulders more widely with shade, and the head doesn't get stuffy. Under a parasol is a little moving space of shade, and you walk through it; even under a strong sun, in the umbrella's shade you feel a little relieved. Of course one hand is taken up — that's the trade-off with a hat: a hat on days heavy with shopping, a parasol on light days; I use them by turns. Once a parasol felt like a women's thing, but not so much lately — many men carry them now; hot as it is, there's no gender to it, and to guard against the heat you use whatever works, a parasol becoming one such ordinary tool. I was a bit self-conscious at first, but once you know the coolness under a parasol, there's no time for shyness. Heat isn't a contest of endurance; you get through summer by fending it off cleverly, and a parasol is a reassuring ally.

Even a searing midsummer road you can manage a bit with this companion shade. Not a flashy tool, just cutting the sunlight; but this one portable shade changes the hardship of a summer outing quite a lot. Today too, I snap the parasol open and step out onto the hot road.
