■ 笹を開ける、あの瞬間

鱒寿司の楽しみは、食べる前から始まっている。丸い容器のふたを開けて、中の笹を、外側から一枚ずつ開いていく。すると、笹の下から、きれいなピンク色の鱒とごはんが、丸い一枚の姿で現れる。

このとき、笹の、青くて少しほろ苦い、いいにおいがふわっと立つ。笹の香りが、鱒とごはんにほんのり移っていて、それが鱒寿司のおいしさの、大事な一部になっている。ただの容器ではなくて、味の一部なのだ。

【English】A round sushi you open leaf by leaf.

The Toyama food best known outside the prefecture is probably masu-zushi — pressed trout sushi. A round bentwood box is lined with bamboo leaf, vinegar-cured trout laid over rice with no gaps, and the whole thing pressed firm. It's sold at station kiosks and is the standard Toyama souvenir. But for those who live here it's less a souvenir than an everyday thing — for memorial gatherings, guests, small celebrations, "let's have masu-zushi today" is ordinary. Here's a note on it.

The pleasure starts before eating. You lift the round lid and peel back the bamboo leaves one by one from the outside, and beneath them a single round disc of pink trout on rice appears. A green, faintly bitter, good smell of bamboo rises — that scent has soaked lightly into the fish and rice, and it's an essential part of the taste. The leaf isn't just packaging; it's part of the flavor.

You cut the round like a cake, radially — with the thin plastic thread that comes with it or a knife, from the center outward into six or eight — and share it around; that sharing suits a gathering. The tang of the vinegar, the fat of the trout, the sweetness of the rice differ a little by maker; Toyama has many shops that make it, each with its own character, and plenty of people have a favorite ("ours is from that place").

Masu-zushi is a pressed sushi, not nigiri: vinegar-cured fish pressed hard together with rice. It's said to come from the wisdom of keeping fish a little longer in an age before refrigeration — trout carried from the sea, cured with vinegar and salt, pressed and wrapped in bamboo. An old workaround has become a regional specialty; food history is full of "a bit of wisdom became a famous dish." Round shape, bamboo scent, pink trout — once seen, remembered; unflashy, but packed with a place's history and ingenuity. I'd be glad if the scent and anticipation of peeling back that leaf came through. (Seasoning and methods vary by shop and home; it's raw fish, so mind storage and dates.)
