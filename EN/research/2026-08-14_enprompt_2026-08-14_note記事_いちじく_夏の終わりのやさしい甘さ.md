■ 皮のまま、かじる

いちじくの食べ方は、家によってまちまちだと思う。うちでは、洗って、へたのあたりを少し落として、皮ごとかじることが多い。皮はうすくて、口の中でほとんど気にならない。むいて食べる人もいるし、縦に四つに割って、中身をスプーンですくう人もいる。どれが正解ということもなくて、たぶん、育った家の流儀がそのまま残っているだけだ。

割ると、中はうすいピンクで、細かいつぶつぶがびっしり詰まっている。あのつぶを噛むと、ぷちぷちと小さくはじけて、舌の上でざらりとした感触が残る。桃のようになめらかでもなく、スイカのようにみずみずしいわけでもない。もっと素朴で、少しもったりした甘さ。強い香りがあるわけではないのに、食べ終わると、口の奥にほんのり残る。

この食感が、子どもの頃はどうも苦手だった。舌にざらつくつぶが、なんだか砂を噛んでいるようで、なめらかな桃のほうがずっと上等に思えた。それがいつの間にか、あのつぶつぶを、わざわざ舌の上で転がして味わうようになっている。歳をとると、なめらかなものより、少し引っかかりのあるもののほうを、おいしいと感じるのかもしれない。理由はうまく説明できないけれど。

【English】"Ichijiku" (fig) — the soft, quiet fruit at the end of summer.

Once mid-August passes, the fruit at the shops shifts a little. The piles of watermelon shrink back, and small purplish fruit, stems up, start lining the shelves — figs. Pick one up and it's softer than you expect; the pad of your finger sinks in slightly. Squeeze hard and it would probably just collapse. That fragility, which the fruit of high summer doesn't have, is somehow dear to me every year.

How you eat them differs by household, I think. At our place we usually rinse them, trim near the stem a bit, and bite in skin and all; the skin is thin and you barely notice it. Some peel them, some quarter them and scoop the inside with a spoon. There's no single right way — probably just the habit of the house you grew up in. Split one open and the inside is pale pink, packed with fine little grains. Bite the grains and they pop softly, leaving a faint rough feel on the tongue — not smooth like a peach, not juicy like watermelon, but plainer, a slightly heavy sweetness.

What figs teach me every year is how quickly they go. Even kept somewhere cool, they slump within a couple of days. Some say the eating-time is when tiny cracks appear on the surface; past that, they turn too soft fast. So at home we try to finish them within two or three days in the fridge, and if anything seems off, we don't force it — soft fruit can be worse inside than it looks. Short season, poor keeping. Maybe that's exactly why I want to taste them while they're around. They aren't a showy fruit, and that plainness suits the end of summer — when the days shorten and cool creeps into mornings and evenings, the quiet sweetness fits the body better than anything loud.
