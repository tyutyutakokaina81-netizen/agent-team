【English】How we use up summer eggplant.

In summer, eggplant piles up — from the garden, from neighbors — and the vegetable drawer fills with glossy dark-purple. It's welcome, but figuring out how to eat it every day is a small daily puzzle. So here are four ways we cook it again and again in our house. Nothing fancy — just an ordinary kitchen on a day when the eggplant has overflowed.

(1) Yakinasu (grilled eggplant): char a whole eggplant until the skin is black — on a fish grill or over the burner — then peel it and eat the soft, melting inside with ginger-soy or bonito flakes. It sounds like effort but it's the simplest, and it tastes the most of eggplant itself: smoky, faintly sweet. Good hot or chilled; some summer nights this and a cold beer are enough.

(2) Miso stir-fry: to use up a lot, cut eggplant in chunks and fry in generous oil (eggplant drinks oil, so don't be shy), then coat with a sauce of miso, sugar and mirin. Add peppers or pork and it's a full dish. The slightly sweet miso makes rice disappear — home cooking in this Hokuriku region is said to run a little sweet, and this is exactly that.

(3) Agebitashi (fried and steeped): deep-fry bite-sized pieces and, while hot, plunge them into a broth of dashi, soy and mirin, then chill. Cold from the fridge is when it's best — the eggplant is meltingly soft and full of dashi. It keeps for a couple of days, which helps on busy ones.

(4) Quick pickles: whatever is still left, slice thin, salt, squeeze out the water; add ginger or myoga for a summery lift. Pickling is the "last catch-all" for over-abundant summer vegetables — cucumber or eggplant, when there's too much, you just salt it and let it sit.

Eggplant overflows in summer — but that's exactly why you can grill it, fry it, steep it, pickle it, and let one vegetable become many things depending on the day. That's the quiet joy of a summer kitchen eating a lot of one seasonal thing. Nothing special — but I'd be glad if a little of how a Japanese home actually eats in summer came through. (Amounts and seasoning vary by household and region; this is just our way. Take care with hot oil and open flame.)
