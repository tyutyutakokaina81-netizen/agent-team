【English】Shiso is all about the fragrance.

Plant shiso (perilla) once and it spreads on its own; by summer the green leaves come faster than you can pick them. In shops a few leaves make a whole pack, but in the garden the problem flips to "how do we use it all?" The best of shiso is its fragrance — heat scatters it, so eating it raw, aroma and all, is ideal. Here are four ways we use it up each summer.

(1) As yakumi (an aromatic garnish) — its most frequent job: shredded over chilled tofu, with somen noodles, beside sashimi, scattered on grilled fish. Just chopping and sprinkling makes a dish feel like summer, and on a day too hot to eat, the fragrance somehow keeps the rice going. With myoga and ginger it's the classic trio of summer garnishes.

(2) Soy-marinated shiso — an "endless" side: layer green leaves in a sauce of soy, a little sesame oil, ground sesame, and garlic or ginger; it's ready in a few hours. Wrap a leaf around warm rice and you won't stop; also good in rice balls, or wrapped around pork and pan-fried. It's how a big pile of shiso disappears deliciously, and it keeps for a few days.

(3) Tempura: coat single leaves thinly and fry quickly. Heat does scatter the aroma, but freshly fried shiso turns nicely toasty instead — thin and fast to fry, sprinkled with a little salt. Fried alongside other summer vegetables like eggplant or squash, it's a whole dinner.

(4) Shiso juice, if you get red shiso: simmer the red leaves, add sugar and lemon or vinegar — the moment the acid goes in, the liquid flips to a bright, clear red. Diluted with water or soda, it's refreshing and kind to a summer-tired body; some households use the red shiso left over from pickling plums.

Shiso is all about fragrance, so you use up the extra leaves in the ways that let the aroma shine — raw garnish, quick pickle, fresh-fried, simmered drink. A whole summer gets a little help from one herb. It can be too much to handle, but that scent is also a treat only summer brings — and I'd be glad if this small summer scene from a Japanese home came through. (Methods vary by household; take care with hot oil and boiling water.)
