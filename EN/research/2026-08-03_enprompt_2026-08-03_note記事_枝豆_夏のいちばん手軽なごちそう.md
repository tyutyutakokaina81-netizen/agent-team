【English】Edamame, summer's easiest treat — straight from the pod.

Come summer, we boil edamame more and more. Salt-boil them in the pod, drain into a colander, then push the beans straight out of the pod into your mouth — that simple a dish, and it's that simplicity that tastes best in the heat. Nothing elaborate: boil with plenty of salt, eat them hot, that's all, and that plainness is just right on a hot day.

Before boiling, rubbing the pods with salt is said to improve color and taste; we usually just put a little extra salt in the boiling water — "maybe too salty?" is about right, and the salt clinging to the pod comes into your mouth as you push the bean out. Doneness divides people — some like them soft, some a firmer bite; we go firm, since overboiling loses that snap. A few minutes, one taste to check, then drain.

You eat them straight from the pod, pushing the bean out with your fingers — not the best manners, but the best taste — sucking a little of the salt left in the pod too. A heaping plate of edamame, and the empty pods pile up as you go; that piling-up is somehow fun, the mountain of beans turning into a mountain of empty pods before you notice, hands and mouth not stopping.

Edamame goes with something cold — beer for the grown-ups, barley tea for the kids — on a hot evening after a bath, drinking something cold and picking at edamame; that's a small pleasure of summer. No special feast: bought at the supermarket, just boiled, yet few things this easy taste this good, so all summer our table sees a lot of it. Fresh from the field is even better — just-picked is said to be sweeter (though views on freshness vary) — but picked or bought, you salt-boil and eat hot, the same either way. It's a plain dish of a Japanese summer, and I'd be glad if this easy treat, pushed straight from the pod, came through a little. (Boiling, salt and timing vary by taste and variety; take care with cooking and food safety, and views on nutrition and freshness differ.)
