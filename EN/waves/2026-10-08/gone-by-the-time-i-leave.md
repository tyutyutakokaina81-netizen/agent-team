# Gone by the Time I Leave

I opened the window and where the river should have been there was white instead. The far bank had disappeared; only the upper part of the houses floated above it. I washed, changed, looked again — thinner already — and by the time I left the house the river was back, with nothing left to prove it had ever been hidden. Nobody I passed on the road mentioned it. Some people saw it and some did not, and for both it was simply the same morning. The explanation I have heard is that it comes after a night that cooled down, but forecasts naming fog have been followed by perfectly clear windows, and white mornings have arrived on days nothing was mentioned. The conditions are apparently not one thing, and past that point I have not looked into it; I find out by opening the window. Even while it is there the thickness is uneven — it pools low, the ridgeline surfaces from the top down, and the last white sits along the river. Whether it goes that way every time, I have not watched long enough to say. This is a place with many rivers and mountains close by, where the days you can see the Tateyama range and the days you cannot are sharply different, and I take the white mornings to be part of that difference — though that is a connection I am making, not one anyone explained to me. When I get one, I feel slightly ahead, and leave the house anyway.
