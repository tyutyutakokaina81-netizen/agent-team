# I Never Actually Chose It

I do not deliberate in front of the shelf. When I buy miso I reach for the same one, and I have no memory of comparing the dozen or so varieties beside it. I could not tell you when it became that one either. It is probably not what my family used; more likely it is whatever I bought first after moving here, and it simply continued. Run out, buy the same, repeat for a few years, and it stops being a choice at all. I know the categories exist — red or white, rice or barley or soybean — but I could not say which one is in my kitchen without reading the tub, and I certainly could not describe the difference in taste to anyone. I eat it every day and have never compared. What I do have an opinion about is the strength: too thin and it feels like nothing, too strong and I get thirsty afterwards, and the band between those is fixed somewhere in me, precise enough that I notice when a bowl falls outside it. I cannot identify the type, but I can judge the dilution. Households here are said to put a lot into the soup — root vegetables, tofu, sometimes fish trimmings — and perhaps all that gives up enough of its own flavour that less miso is needed. Writing that, I realise I have never checked it either. Standing at the miso shelf without hesitating is probably just never having felt the need to hesitate.
