# Clothes Change on a Date, Not a Temperature

The first of June and the first of October. On those two dates school uniforms switch over — by the calendar, not the thermometer. In the original form you went into long sleeves on 1 October however warm it was, and into short sleeves on 1 June however cold. That has loosened: many schools and workplaces now allow a transition period of some weeks where either is acceptable, and at the end of September you see children still in short sleeves walking beside others already in long ones. The old sight of everyone changing at once has, at least as far as I can see, mostly gone. The dates themselves remain, though — loosened, but still the reference point. The household version has no deadline, and so it never finishes. The summer things do not get put away, November arrives, and the winter clothes come out on top of them; some years the cupboard holds both seasons at once. It is the kind of job that only happens if you decide today is the day, which makes the uniform system, with its fixed date, the more sensible arrangement. What I get stuck on is the clothes I never wore. Folding away a summer shirt that never got used, I think I probably will not wear it next year either — and I keep it anyway, and have the same thought at the next changeover. That round trip happens twice a year. Here in Toyama some Octobers stay hot and some Novembers want the heating on, so the timing is hard to read. And because this is snow country the winter half is bulky: coats, thick socks, boots. Less an exchange than a matter of the room getting smaller once the winter things are out.
