【English】Yakisoba — that smell of sauce on a day off.

When that smell of sauce drifts from the kitchen, your stomach starts to growl. On the frying pan, noodles, ingredients and sauce make a good sizzling sound; that smell of scorched sauce, sweet and savory — why does it make you so hungry? On a day off at noon, that smell somehow lifts the mood a little. Yakisoba is no grand dish: fry cabbage and pork, add the noodles, coat with sauce — that's all, no special ingredients needed, made from whatever's in the fridge. But that ease and that taste are just right for a day-off lunch.

Say yakisoba and many think first of a summer festival stall — that scene of great quantities cooked boldly on a big iron plate, the sauce smell wafting all around the stall; catch it and you end up buying some. But home-made yakisoba is good in its own way — no festival boldness, but for that you can put in the ingredients you like, as much as you like. We go heavy on pork, plenty of cabbage, and the sauce a touch strong, to taste; that you can adjust it to your own liking is the good of home yakisoba. The finishing touches hold their own pleasures: first a shake of aonori, then a side of pickled red ginger — just a little of that red ginger and the taste tightens right up — and some add bonito flakes or mayonnaise.

Our small luxury is to top it with a fried egg — a soft one on the yakisoba, breaking the yolk with a pchin and coating the noodles, the runny yolk mixing into the rich sauce, irresistible; that one extra step turns the usual yakisoba a little into a feast. Now and then you just crave yakisoba, though it's no elaborate dish — that rich, sauce-flavored, humble-grub feeling; sometimes you long for it badly. People can't live on refined dishes alone; sometimes you need that punch of sauce. On a day off at noon, you fry yakisoba sizzling on the pan, the sauce smell spreading through the house — not a special day at all, just an ordinary day-off lunch, yet that ordinary plate is somehow quietly good, one small pleasure of a summer day off. (Ingredients, seasoning and toppings vary by taste; this is our one example and points to no particular product or shop. Mind cooking and food safety; a soft egg is to taste — mind freshness and your condition.)
