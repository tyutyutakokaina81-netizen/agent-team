【English】Water guns — a soaking battle.

You take aim and pull the trigger hard, and a thin line of water flies out — byuu — a hit on the enemy's back. "Whoa, cold!" a shriek goes up, and instantly return fire flies at you; fleeing, you shoot back. A summer yard's water-gun battle: once it starts, there's no stopping it, and before you know it everyone's soaked. A water gun is a simple toy — put water in, shoot it out, that's all; yet just shooting water is, in summer, somehow irresistibly fun. Splashing each other with cold water, shrieking and fleeing — precisely because it's a hot day, getting wet feels good; the water gun is a magic tool that turns the heat itself into play.

The battle starts as play, but as you go, everyone gradually gets serious: a gun that shoots farther, a gun that holds more water — the weapon's performance decides the win, and the kid with the big pump-type dominates the field. Run out of water and there's a truce, everyone dashing to the bucket or hose to refill — that truce during the resupply; but resupply done, the battle resumes: aim, shoot, flee, shoot again, and the children, never tiring, repeat this chase of water endlessly. At first it's just the kids, but before long the grown-ups get pulled in: "hey, don't spray this way," and water flies the moment you say it, and the grown-up who dodged at first gradually gets worked up, and before they know it is gripping a water gun right alongside the kids. A grown adult, in dead earnest, splashing water with a child — from the outside, what a silly sight it must be. But that silliness is the good of it: on a hot day, minding not the wet, everyone romping, that time of soaked-through play without a shred of reserve — that can only be had in summer.

After shooting it out to the last, everyone soaked, so wet there's no way to get wetter, the battle finally ends; in sopping clothes, everyone laughing, catching their breath, and a summer breeze on the wet body feels a little cool. No great game — just splashing water; yet that memory of getting soaked and laughing with your whole body somehow stays forever. The feel of that cold water and everyone's laughter — that may have been the simplest and gladdest play of summer. (Water guns are water play; this is our one example and memory. Don't shoot at faces or eyes, don't slip and fall on wet ground — mind injury; take care with water accidents and heatstroke too, and enjoy within reason. Impressions vary.)
