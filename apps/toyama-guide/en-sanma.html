Autumn's Fish, Except Where There Are Others

I watched several people walk straight past the boxes of saury at the fishmonger, which is a slightly odd thing to see — saury is the autumn fish in Japan. Plenty of people do buy it. It is just that in Toyama it is not the only candidate for the title.

The fish itself

Sanma is a slim, oily fish, and the standard treatment is salt and a whole-fish grilling: head and tail left on, guts left in, eaten bitter parts and all, with grated daikon and soy sauce alongside.

Visitors are usually startled by two things. That the fish arrives entire. And that the bitter innards are deliberately kept — treating bitterness as part of the flavour rather than a fault is hard to explain to someone who did not grow up with it.

Catches have been poor in recent years, from what I hear, and the price is higher than it used to be. Sea temperature and changed migration get mentioned; I cannot vouch for the reasons. What does seem true is that "the autumn when saury is cheap" is no longer something to count on.

The Toyama part

The bay here is deep, and what it yields changes with the season: whelk in autumn, oval squid around now, filefish once it cools a little, with red snow crab and yellowtail waiting further on. So nationally saury may be the autumn fish, but on this counter it does not quite take the lead.

Not because it is worse — because there is more to choose from. Somewhere inland, saury would be the season in a single fish. Here it competes. That is, I admit, a spoiled way to see it; a friend from elsewhere was visibly surprised when I said "saury will do tonight."

The grilling is the same everywhere

High heat, skin blackening, fat dripping, smoke. Run the extractor and the smell stays in the room anyway, and that smell is more or less what an autumn evening means. On a charcoal grill outdoors the skin chars differently, which costs you some consideration towards the neighbours about the smoke.

Draw the Japanese autumn table as a single picture and saury is at the centre of it. Real tables vary by where you are standing, and Toyama's comes out busier than the picture. A picture and the thing itself diverge most places, probably.

Fact-check note
  • ✅ Pacific saury (sanma) is regarded as a representative autumn fish in Japan; salt-grilling it whole is the common preparation.
  • ✅ Grilling with the innards left in, and eating the bitterness, is widely done; grated daikon is the standard accompaniment.
  • ✅ Toyama Bay is deep and yields different local fish by season; whelk, oval squid, filefish, red snow crab and yellowtail are all known catches there.
  • ⚠️ The seasons for those species shift with the year and sea conditions, so the article gives them loosely ("in autumn", "once it cools a little").
  • ⚠️ "Poor catches and higher prices in recent years" is given as what one hears; no figures and no cause are claimed.
  • ⚠️ "It does not take the lead on this counter" is the writer's lived impression, not a statistic.
  • ⚠️ Tolerance for the bitterness varies between people; no claim is made about everyone in Japan.
  • ⚠️ Smoke from outdoor grilling requires consideration for neighbours — stated rather than glossed over.
