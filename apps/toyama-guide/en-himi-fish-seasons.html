Himi's 5 AM Fish Auction, and What's in Season Month by Month

Himi isn't a sightseeing town so much as a working fishing port, and its clock is set by the sea. At 5 AM the boats return, the auction begins, buyers carry off the catch, and by noon that fish is on a counter or a conveyor-belt sushi plate. If you want to understand Himi, don't start with a landmark—start with what the boats brought in this morning.

The morning rhythm

Arrive early and you'll see it: boats coming in, fish laid out and hosed down, the auction's quick calling, buyers hauling away crates. Some of the port is viewable and some isn't—ask staff where visitors can watch, and check before photographing. The short gap between catch and plate is the whole point: elsewhere "fresh" means a day or two in transit; in Himi the morning's fish can be lunch. That's why local diners can serve raw fish from the opening hour.

The seasonal catch calendar

  • April–May — firefly squid (hotaruika): a spring signature of Toyama Bay, translucent, faintly sweet, often served boiled.
  • June–August — white shrimp (shiroebi): delicate, pale, sweet; as sashimi it lingers on the tongue.
  • September–November — yellowtail (buri): a fish that fattens as it grows; autumn is when locals say the flavor is at its best.
  • December–March — nodoguro (rosy seabass): prized and pricey elsewhere, here caught overnight and eaten the same day.

Knowing the season tells you what to order. Guidebooks fix Himi as "white shrimp," but "the catch changes with the season" is closer to the truth of the place.

No guarantees: the port runs on weather

There's no promise you'll eat a specific fish on a specific day. Bad weather cancels the boats, so there's no auction and the "today's fresh ◯◯" board disappears—frozen stock fills in. Seasons shift too: a slow rise in water temperature can push a run back by days. Come willing to eat what the sea decided, and Himi rewards you; come with a fixed checklist and it may frustrate you. That surrender is the experience.

How to eat like a local

Skip the most touristed counter and sit where locals ask the owner "what's good today." For a first visit, conveyor-belt sushi lets you sample the whole day's range cheaply; a set-meal diner's donburi (rice bowl) piles on the morning's catch. Eat breakfast, lunch, and the next morning here and the menu may differ each time—that's the port, not a flaw. A full sashimi meal often runs less than in a big city, and fresher.

Fact-check note
  • ✅ Himi's fishing port and morning auction are real and operating; the 5 AM timing depends on season, weather, and the day's fishing.
  • ✅ Firefly squid, white shrimp, yellowtail, and nodoguro are real principal catches — abundance varies with the season.
  • ⚠️ "Caught overnight, eaten the same day" depends on distance and cold-chain; not every diner does this, and some use frozen stock.
  • ⚠️ Seasonal windows are general tendencies; exact timing shifts with water temperature and the year. No guarantee of a specific fish on a specific day.
