Japan's Autumn Colour Does Not Wait for November

The foliage photographs that travel are from Kyoto in November, and on the flat that is roughly right — that is when the trees turn. In Toyama, though, there are places where it begins in September, and they are the high ones.

Six weeks apart, in one prefecture

The upper reaches of the Tateyama range sit above two thousand metres, cold enough that the season runs to a different schedule. While the foot of the mountains is still in short sleeves, the vegetation up top has started changing colour.

Rowan is generally said to redden first — a low shrub carrying red berries — and after it the grassland shifts through yellow into red-brown, while creeping pine holds its green in between. A single slope can carry all three at once. When it peaks moves around: late September in some years, into October in others, weather depending. There is no date that guarantees it.

Two seasons in one day

What makes the place unusual is that a day's climb walks you through the calendar. Summer lingering at the bottom, autumn partway up, a hint of winter at the top — compressed, because Toyama runs from sea level to three thousand metres over a short horizontal distance.

It is not all upside. It is cold up there. Strong wind in September can call for winter kit, and when the weather turns the view simply goes; people underdress for it every year. Colour arriving early means the cold arrives early too.

Autumn, twice

The lowland has its own turn around November. Living here, that means watching autumn twice — once on the mountain in September, once where you actually live about six weeks later. The calendar calls both of them autumn; the altitude disagrees.

Most visitors aim at November on the flat, and that is beautiful in its own right. But the red you can see at the end of September sits about an hour above a valley that still feels like summer.

Fact-check note
  • ✅ High mountain zones colour earlier than the lowlands, and Japan's alpine areas including Tateyama are known for early foliage.
  • ✅ The Tateyama range includes peaks from roughly 2,000 m to about 3,000 m.
  • ✅ Rowan (red), yellowing grassland and creeping pine (green) appear together on alpine slopes.
  • ⚠️ "Rowan reddens first" is given as the generally noted tendency.
  • ⚠️ Peak timing varies considerably year to year; no date is claimed and the article says as much.
  • ⚠️ "About six weeks" and "about an hour" are rough figures that depend on the year, the route and where you start.
  • ⚠️ Cold, wind, kit and lost visibility are stated as real drawbacks — early colour means early cold.
