Japan's Autumn Colour Does Not Wait for November

The foliage photographs that travel are from Kyoto in November, and on the flat that is roughly right — that is when the trees turn. In Toyama, though, there are places where it begins in September, and they are the high ones.

Six weeks apart, in one prefecture

The upper reaches of the Tateyama range sit above two thousand metres, cold enough that the season runs to a different schedule. While the foot of the mountains is still in short sleeves, the vegetation up top has started changing colour.

Rowan of one kind or another is generally said to redden first — it carries red berries, and grows low at that altitude — and after it the grassland shifts through yellow into red-brown, while creeping pine holds its green in between. A single slope can carry all three at once. When it peaks moves around: late September in some years, into October in others, weather depending. There is no date that guarantees it.

Early colour is early cold

It is not all upside. It is cold up there. Strong wind in September can call for winter kit, and when the weather turns the view simply goes; going up underdressed and regretting it is a story you hear often enough.

Colour arriving early is really just cold arriving early. What looks beautiful is a plant already preparing for winter, which has nothing to do with the convenience of anyone looking at it.

Autumn, twice

The lowland has its own turn around November. Living here, that means watching autumn twice — once on the mountain in September, once where you actually live about six weeks later. The calendar calls both of them autumn; the altitude disagrees.

Most visitors aim at November on the flat, and that is beautiful in its own right. But about two months before it, higher up in the same prefecture, a first autumn is already finishing — on days when the valley below still feels like summer.

Fact-check note
  • ✅ High mountain zones colour earlier than the lowlands, and Japan's alpine areas including Tateyama are known for early foliage.
  • ✅ The Tateyama range includes peaks from roughly 2,000 m to about 3,000 m.
  • ✅ Rowan (red), yellowing grassland and creeping pine (green) appear together on alpine slopes.
  • ⚠️ "Rowan reddens first" is given as the generally noted tendency. Rowan proper is a small tree of roughly 7–10 m; the low-growing plants at altitude are usually related alpine species, so no single species is named here.
  • ⚠️ Peak timing varies considerably year to year; no date is claimed and the article says as much.
  • ⚠️ "About six weeks" and "about two months" are rough figures that depend on the year and the place; no date is given.
  • ⚠️ Cold, wind, kit and lost visibility are stated as real drawbacks — early colour means early cold.
