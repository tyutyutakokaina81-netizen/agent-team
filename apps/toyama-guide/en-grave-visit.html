How Often a Family Visits the Grave Is Not Fixed

How many times a year people here go to the family grave varies from household to household. The spring and autumn equinox weeks, the Obon period in August, the end of the year — some families go for all of them, some only in August. There is no set number.

What you take, and how long it takes

Flowers, incense, water. Buckets and ladles are usually already there at the site.

The work is short. Pull the weeds around the plot, pour water over the stone, put the flowers in, light the incense, put your hands together. Fifteen minutes, if that.

For those fifteen minutes you drive out, park, and drive back. Most of the time it takes is travel, which is true of a lot of Japanese observances.

Nobody taught me the procedure

How many sticks of incense, how to pour the water, which flowers to bring — that apparently differs by Buddhist sect and by region.

I have never been formally taught any of it. I do what I watched my parents do, without knowing whether it is right. There are people I could ask, and on the occasions I have, the answer is that I should not worry about it.

So this page is not a how-to. It is one household's version, and I would not offer it as the correct one.

The autumn equinox

In September the heat often lingers, and the weeds are still growing well.

The ones pulled in August are back at exactly the same height. That is how I register that two months have gone by — grass being a more reliable calendar than the one on the wall.

In Toyama

Distances vary a lot. Within the same prefecture, the grave can be an hour's drive from the house.

Family who have moved away tend to go when they are back for the holidays. So the equinox and Obon are less about the grave's schedule than about the days when people are in town.

And more than once I have settled where we are eating afterwards before we set off. The hands together and the lunch end up in the same day.

Fact-check note
  • ✅ Grave visits around the spring and autumn equinox, Obon and year-end are a widely known custom in Japan.
  • ✅ The autumn equinox week falls around the equinox itself in late September.
  • ⚠️ Frequency and timing differ by household, as the opening says. No standard is implied.
  • ⚠️ Procedure differs by sect and region, and this page deliberately does not prescribe one. Even pouring water over the stone is viewed differently in different traditions.
  • ⚠️ "Family who moved away tend to go at the holidays" is the writer's impression of their surroundings, not a statistic.
  • ⚠️ "An hour's drive within the prefecture" illustrates the range; no specific place is meant.
  • ⚠️ No temple, cemetery, district or personal name appears (prefecture level only).
