The Paddies Turn Green a Second Time

A few weeks after the harvest, fields that were finished come back.

Once the rice is cut, what remains is rows of brown stubble. That is how the paddies look for a while.

It comes back

Then new leaves push out of the cut stumps.

From a distance the whole field reads faintly green — nothing like the tall green before cutting. Low, sparse, pale. Up close you can see a few blades rising from each stump.

This regrowth is called niban-bo. Some people say hikobae. The word varies by region and I have heard both.

It never fills

Heads sometimes form. Here they do not reach the point of filling.

The temperature drops faster than the grain can set. The stalks dry standing, and then the snow arrives. It is not harvested.

I have read that warmer regions do cut this second growth. That is not something people talk about in this part of the country.

Green for no one

So the green belongs to nobody.

Not grown to be eaten, not grown to be sold — simply the stumps still being alive. A state of "there is more, after the point of being finished" sits on the fields for several weeks.

In Toyama

Once October starts, mornings increasingly bring mist over the paddies. For a short while, white mist rests on that thin green.

By November the preparations for winter begin. Some years the snow comes down on top of it while it is still green. The second growth is never harvested — but being buried is part of the yearly picture too.

Fact-check note
  • ✅ Shoots that regrow from cut rice stumps are called hikobae or niban-bo (also hitsuji).
  • ✅ In warm regions there are cases of harvesting the regrowth (ratoon cropping).
  • ⚠️ The names vary regionally. The text presents this as something heard rather than a rule.
  • ⚠️ "Here it does not reach the point of filling" is what the writer has seen; growing conditions across the region were not researched.
  • ⚠️ The writer is not a farmer. How the stumps are handled after cutting is an agricultural decision the text does not claim to know.
  • ⚠️ The October mist and November preparations are the writer's impression, not statistics. Timing shifts from year to year.
  • ⚠️ No dates or snowfall figures are given, because year-to-year variation is large.
  • ⚠️ No district, farm or personal name appears (prefecture level only).
