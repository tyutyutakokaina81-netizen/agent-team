Only the Hour It Gets Dark Keeps Sliding

Of everything in a day, the part that moves is the hour it goes dark.

The mornings barely register — probably because I get up at a fixed time. The evenings shift, because whatever I am doing finishes at a different point each day, and the light outside when I step out gives it away.

Late September moves fast

Past the equinox the nights here are longer than the days, and this is one of the stretches when sunset moves fastest through the calendar.

Last week it was bright at a given hour; this week it is already dim. A difference you can feel within a single week only happens in spring and autumn.

The planning does not keep up

For a while the evening still gets organised as though it were August.

Go out for the shopping and come back in the dark. Leave the washing out. Forget the bicycle light and notice halfway along. The timetable in my head runs about a fortnight behind the actual sky.

By October I have adjusted — and by then sunset has moved into the five o'clock hour anyway.

The dark looks different, too

Summer nights stay somehow hazy, perhaps from the heat still sitting in the air.

By autumn the air has dried, and the street lights and traffic signals look harder-edged, more separate from their surroundings. That is an impression rather than physics, and I offer it as one.

In Toyama

The coast and the inland differ. From the sea side you watch the sun go down to the horizon and see the whole thing happen.

Inland the sky behind you darkens first, and the mountain range takes the rest. The actual sunset time can hardly differ across a single prefecture, yet some places feel dark noticeably sooner than others.

Fact-check note
  • ✅ After the autumn equinox, nights in Japan are longer than days.
  • ✅ The weeks around the equinoxes are when sunrise and sunset times change fastest in the year.
  • ⚠️ "A difference you can feel within a week" is the writer's experience; no figures in minutes are given, as they vary by year and location.
  • ⚠️ "Sunset moves into the five o'clock hour by October" is approximate and shifts with location and year. Check an almanac for exact times.
  • ⚠️ The difference in how the dark "looks" between summer and autumn is stated in the text as an impression, not a physical explanation.
  • ⚠️ Coast and inland differ in how the sunset is seen, which is a matter of terrain — not a claim that sunset times differ meaningfully within the prefecture.
  • ⚠️ No district, facility or personal name appears (prefecture level only).
