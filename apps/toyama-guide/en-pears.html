In Toyama, You Bite a Pear Like an Apple

From late summer into early autumn, pears pile up at Toyama's roadside stands and farm shops — round, heavy, and nothing like what a visitor might expect. If you picture a European pear (soft, buttery, ripened until it melts) and bite into a Japanese one, you're in for a surprise. The Japanese pear — nashi, or wanashi — is shaped like a round apple, pale inside, and astonishingly full of water. You don't wait for it to soften; you eat it firm and chilled, and it snaps with an audible crunch, juice bursting in your mouth.

A different fruit from the European pear

In English, "pear" usually means the Western pear: gourd-shaped, ripened after picking until soft and fragrant, eaten melting. The Japanese pear is the opposite — apple-round, brown-skinned, white and dense inside, and extremely juicy. You chill it and eat it hard, cut into wedges. Same word, near-opposite texture. That clean crunch comes from tiny grains in the flesh (called stone cells); rather than any grittiness, they give the fruit its signature crisp bite.

Toyama has pear country too

Some other prefectures are more famous for pears, but Toyama has long-standing pear-growing areas of its own. Early varieties arrive at the end of summer, giving way to later ones through September. Locally, the day's fruit is sold by the box at farm shops and roadside stands — usually bigger, cheaper, and juicier than what reaches a city supermarket. Earlier varieties tend to be light and refreshing, later ones sweeter and fuller of juice — though it shifts with the year's weather. Locals gauge the season by taste: "this year's are really sweet," "we had a lot of rain this year."

Chill it, slice it, eat it as is

The way to eat a Japanese pear is simple: chill it well, peel, cut into wedges, and eat it plain. It's rarely cooked or used in a dish. A well-chilled pear on a still-warm September day — that cold, crisp, dripping bite is the taste of summer ending in Toyama. If you meet this fruit in a Japanese autumn, don't imagine a European pear. Just take a bite. Expecting softness, you'll be happily wrong.

Fact-check note
  • ✅ Japanese pears (nashi) are round, very juicy, and crisp; eaten firm and chilled, raw.
  • ✅ European pears are a different type, ripened soft after picking — a big texture difference.
  • ✅ The crisp texture comes from "stone cells" in the flesh.
  • ✅ Toyama has long-standing pear-growing areas; fruit is sold direct from late summer into autumn.
  • ⚠️ "Early varieties light, later ones sweeter" is a general tendency; it shifts by variety and the year's weather.
  • ⚠️ Specific varieties and sugar levels vary by year and grower, so they're left general here.
