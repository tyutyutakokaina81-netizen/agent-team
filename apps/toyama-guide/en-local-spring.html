Travel · Toyama, Japan · Spring

A local's spring in Toyama

Spring is the season Toyama stops being a secret. The snow that buried the mountains all winter becomes a wall you can walk between, the bay lights up at night, and the blossom finally arrives — late, this far north, and all the better against the white peaks. This is the everyday version from someone who grew up in Takaoka, and the fourth part of the year alongside summer, autumn and winter.

The snow corridor opens

Spring's headline act is the Tateyama Kurobe Alpine Route reopening, usually from mid-April. Snowploughs cut a road straight through the winter's accumulation, leaving walls of snow that in the highest stretch can tower many metres over the bus — the famous snow corridor (yuki-no-otani). It is the one spring sight that genuinely stops people in their tracks, and it's busiest at the very start of the season. Because it's all about altitude and snowmelt, opening dates and how high the walls stand vary by year, so check the Alpine Route calendar before you plan around it.

The bay that glows at night

Down at sea level, spring brings something stranger: firefly squid (hotaruika). For a few weeks, roughly March into May, these small squid come up to spawn in Toyama Bay, and they glow — a faint blue light. On certain nights the shoreline shimmers with them, and they're a seasonal delicacy too, boiled and eaten with a sharp miso dressing. It's one of those things that sounds made up until you see it. (Timing and whether the glow is visible on any given night depend on the tide and the year.)

Blossom, late and worth the wait

This far north on the Sea of Japan side, the cherry blossom comes later than Tokyo or Kyoto — often early to mid-April — which is a quiet advantage: if you missed the blossom further south, it may still be waiting here. The local trick is the backdrop. Blossom is everywhere in Japan; blossom with a wall of 3,000-metre snow peaks behind it is a Toyama thing. Add the first white shrimp of the year coming into the bay, and the table starts to lighten after winter's heavy food.

The everyday feel of the thaw

For people who live here, spring is mostly relief. The snow that had to be shovelled all winter finally goes, the roads clear, and the light comes back. It lines up with the start of Japan's school and working year in April, so there's a sense of things beginning — new uniforms at the station, rice paddies filling with water and mirroring the sky before planting. None of it is dramatic. It's just the year turning over, gently.

When to come, and how

Aim for mid-April to May: the Alpine Route is open, the snow walls are at their tallest early on, firefly squid season overlaps, and the blossom lands in the first half of April. It's one of the better-value windows, though the Alpine Route's opening days are busy — plan ahead. Toyama is about 15 minutes from Kanazawa by shinkansen, an easy day trip from Kanazawa. A note on honesty: this is one resident's view, not a definitive account. Opening dates, blossom timing, snow-wall height and the firefly-squid glow all shift year to year and depend on weather and tides — treat this as a starting point, and check current dates and forecasts before you travel.
