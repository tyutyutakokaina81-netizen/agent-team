Scarecrows With Faces, and Without

Scarecrows in the paddies here come in two kinds: the ones with a face, and the ones without. Both can be standing in the same field.

Two levels of effort

The first sort has eyes and a mouth on a round cloth. Somebody sewed that cloth, drew the face, and in some cases set the angle of the straw hat with obvious care.

The second is a pole in an old shirt with a hat dropped on top, the sleeves pinned out sideways — the look of something put up in the middle of another job. From a distance it still reads as a human shape.

Which of the two works better I have no idea. I do not know what the birds make of either.

Does it work at all

A kakashi is there to keep birds and animals off the crop. It is also said to lose its effect once they get used to it, standing in the same spot week after week.

I have seen a bird perched on a scarecrow's shoulder. On that particular day I felt rather sorry for it.

These days more fields use reflective tape, netting, or an electric fence. The scarecrow stands among all that as a single leftover figure.

After the harvest

Once the rice is cut, the field is nothing but stubble — and some of them are still out there in it.

They guard nothing now, and they stay until somebody takes them down. Others have fallen. A fallen scarecrow is a human shape lying flat in an empty field, which is faintly unsettling in a way the standing ones are not.

In Toyama

Drive around at this time of year and you pass cut field after cut field, with one or two still upright in them.

The ones with faces have usually had the colour rained out of their clothes — up since spring, I assume. The least useful stretch of their year is the one they spend standing longest.

Fact-check note
  • ✅ Kakashi are put up in fields to deter birds and animals.
  • ✅ Their effect is regarded as limited; animals are said to habituate to a scarecrow that does not move. This page does not claim they are useless or effective.
  • ✅ Reflective tape, netting and electric fences are widely used in Japan.
  • ⚠️ "More fields use other methods" is the writer's impression, not a measured proportion.
  • ⚠️ The two-kinds split is an observation, not an established classification.
  • ⚠️ The bird on the shoulder is one thing the writer saw; it proves nothing about effectiveness.
