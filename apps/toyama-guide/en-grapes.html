The Part Where You Spit Out the Seeds Has Gone

Somewhere along the way I stopped doing something: taking grape seeds out of my mouth.

As a child you put the grape in, and the skin and the seed came back out onto the edge of the plate, where a small pile built up over the course of a bunch. Now I chew and swallow, skin and all.

Nobody announced the change

Seedless varieties you eat whole are what the shops mostly carry now. No peeling, no seeds, no sticky fingers, no plate required.

I do not remember consciously accepting this. At some point the grapes in the house were simply that kind, and the old way of eating had stopped.

What made me notice was eating a seeded variety again after a long gap, and having to pause over what you were supposed to do with them.

Whether anything was lost, I can't say

Less work is better, presumably, and I am not going to argue otherwise.

Still — during the peeling and the spitting I was probably thinking about something else. That stretch of time is now gone. Whether it counts as a loss is not something I can settle.

The flavour has not got worse. If anything it is sweeter. This is not an argument that things tasted better before.

The expensive ones

Premium varieties here are very expensive, and the boxed gift ones put me off entirely.

I buy the ordinary sort once or twice each autumn and that is enough. I have eaten the expensive kind, but not in a way I remember well enough to describe the difference.

In Toyama

Grapes are grown in parts of the prefecture, and they appear at the roadside produce stands in season.

But I rarely hear anyone say "Toyama grapes" the way they talk about the rice or the fish. The fruit here — pears included — somehow never got attached to the name of the place, though it is certainly being grown.
