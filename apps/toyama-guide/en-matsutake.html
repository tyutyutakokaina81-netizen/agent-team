I Have Never Bought One, and I Know the Name Anyway

I have looked at the price and put it back on the shelf. More than once.

Matsutake appears in shops every autumn. I have never bought one with my own money.

You cannot start anywhere but the price

Domestic matsutake is expensive — a few mushrooms can cost what several meals out would.

Imported ones are more reachable, arriving from China, Canada, the United States, Turkey and elsewhere. The country of origin is printed on the label, and that label is where a lot of people first learn matsutake comes from more than one place.

Domestic production is said to have declined over a long period. The reason usually given is that the red pine woodland it grows in stopped being maintained: people no longer go in for firewood and leaf litter, and the state of the woods changed. Whether that is the main cause, I am not able to judge.

Aroma, supposedly, is the point

It is described as being about aroma rather than flavour, and the standard treatments — grilled and torn apart, in a clear broth, cooked into rice — all point that way.

Honestly, I do not know that aroma well. I know the smell of soup powders and of products flavoured to resemble it. Whether that matches the real thing I could not say, never having compared them.

Thinking you know a taste when what you know is the manufactured version is not unique to this.

Nobody says where

It grows in the hills, and the people who pick it do not say where.

Not out of spite. Woodland has owners, and going in uninvited causes problems. Beyond that, a spot that becomes known stops producing, so the information stays inside a small circle — sometimes not even the whole family.

There are mountains in Toyama and somebody is presumably picking up there, but none has ever reached me. Akebi and other mushrooms travel hand to hand down to lowland kitchens; matsutake alone does not move that way.

The name comes first

As autumn's luxury item, matsutake is known by name before it is known at all. The price, the aroma, the decline — I grew up hearing all three and have never once chosen one myself.

What I know is not the smell of matsutake. It is the smell of the thing that stands in for it.

  • ✅ Matsutake is valued primarily for aroma; grilling, clear broth and cooking into rice are standard preparations.
  • ✅ Picking spots are customarily kept secret, and woodland has owners who must be respected.

  • ⚠️ "I do not know the aroma well" is the writer's own admission, not an assessment of how real and imitation aromas compare.
  • ⚠️ "A known spot stops producing" is reported as something heard, not as a quantified effect of picking pressure.
  • ⚠️ Entering or foraging in private woodland without permission causes problems — stated in the text. This is not a guide to picking.
