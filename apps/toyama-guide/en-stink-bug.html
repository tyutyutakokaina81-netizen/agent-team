The Insect That Is Supposed to Know About Snow

It does not bite, and it does not go after food.

Bring the laundry in during autumn and one comes in with it. The only real problem is that handling it wrong leaves the room smelling.

Everyone has a method

The first rule is agreed on: do not hit it.

After that the methods diverge. Onto a sheet of paper and out the window. Dropped into a plastic bottle and capped. Vacuumed up — which is said to leave a smell afterwards, and neatly divides people into those who do it and those who refuse.

On a good day it leaves without incident. On a bad one it stays on your hands for a while.

Some years there are many

Some autumns they line up along the window frame; other years you barely see one.

In a heavy year you start checking the washing a piece at a time before bringing it in. In a light year you forget you ever did. You notice you forgot when the next heavy year arrives.

It gets tied to the snow

A year with many of them means a winter with heavy snow, people say.

Winter comes up early in conversation in this part of the country, so anything abundant in autumn tends to get read as a sign. Whether it holds I could not tell you. The years it matched are the ones that stayed in memory; nobody keeps count of the years it did not.

In Toyama

Snow here decides how the whole season has to be arranged.

So wanting to forecast it makes sense to me. Even now, with proper weather reports available, the saying still gets said — not out of distrust of the forecast, I think. Just wanting one more opinion.

Whether this year counts as many or few, I will reach November without having decided.

Fact-check note
  • ✅ Stink bugs seek overwintering shelter in autumn and enter buildings; they release an odour when disturbed.
  • ⚠️ "Many stink bugs means heavy snow" is folklore, not an established forecasting method. The text says outright that its accuracy is unknown.
  • ⚠️ "Vacuuming leaves a smell" is repeated hearsay and depends on the machine and situation.
  • ⚠️ "Numbers vary by year" is the writer's impression; no population data was consulted.
  • ⚠️ No species is identified — many species enter houses, and which ones varies by region and year.
  • ⚠️ No removal chemicals or methods are recommended; that is a safety and legal matter the text does not enter.
  • ⚠️ No data linking Toyama snowfall to insect numbers was consulted. The text only reports that the saying is still spoken.
  • ⚠️ No district, household or address is identified (prefecture level only).
