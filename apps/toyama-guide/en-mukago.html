Too Small to Make a Dish

What you can gather at one time does not add up to a portion.

Mukago are the small bulbils that form where the leaf meets the vine on Japanese yam plants. By autumn they turn brown and drop at a touch.

It never becomes a plate of anything

The biggest are about the size of a thumb tip. The smallest are under a pea.

Gather them and you have a handful, and a handful is where it stops — not enough to stir-fry, not enough to simmer. So they go into the rice. Mukago gohan is, I suspect, the dish that stuck precisely because nothing else was possible.

You do not peel them. Wash them, add a little salt, cook them with the rice. People describe the texture as sticky; I would put it closer to eating a very small potato.

Some years they are sold, some years not

They turn up at farm stands in little bags, priced above the other vegetables.

Other years they do not turn up at all. You buy them if you see them rather than going out to find them, and I cannot remember seeing them in a supermarket.

I do not pick them

Vines do hang over the paths where the hills meet the flat land. But I do not know whose land it is, so I leave them alone.

There are also similar-looking vines I could not reliably tell apart. That is the honest reason as much as the first one.

In Toyama

The mountains sit immediately behind the plain here, so the vines themselves are a common sight.

And yet mukago remain something that someone else gathers and a farm stand sells a little of — not something that reaches the table every year. Among autumn foods, they are the one whose defining trait is that the quantity never accumulates.

Fact-check note
  • ✅ Mukago are bulbils formed on the vines of plants in the Japanese yam genus, at the leaf axils. They ripen and fall in autumn.
  • ✅ Cooking them whole with rice (mukago gohan) is a widely known preparation.
  • ⚠️ Size and yield vary by plant and by year. "About the size of a thumb tip" is what the writer has seen, not a standard.
  • ⚠️ The descriptions of taste and texture are the writer's impression, not measurement.
  • ⚠️ "Some years they are sold and some years not" is what the writer has observed; no distribution data was consulted.
  • ⚠️ Foraging requires the permission of the landowner or manager. The text says the writer does not pick them.
  • ⚠️ Some vines resembling Japanese yam are not edible. If you cannot tell them apart with confidence, do not gather or eat them. The text states the writer cannot.
