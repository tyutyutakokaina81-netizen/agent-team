The Tears Are Always at the Same Height

About fifty centimetres from the floor.

A hand went out to steady someone, or something knocked into it, and that is where the paper goes. The upper panes stay white for years while the bottom row gives way one square at a time.

Repair has stages

Few households re-paper the whole screen the moment it tears.

The first response is a patch over the single broken pane — you can buy repair paper die-cut into cherry-blossom shapes, so the tear becomes a flower. The next stage up is re-papering that one row. Doing the entire screen comes later than both.

The flowers accumulate

Once there are two or three blossoms on one screen, nobody reads them as damage any more.

What began as a stopgap starts behaving like a pattern. That is probably why the full re-papering keeps getting postponed.

You pick the day yourself

There is no season for it.

Some families do it at the end of the year, some wait for a mild day. The glue needs air while it dries, so the house goes cold if you pick a cold day, and summer is too humid for it to dry well. Which leaves whichever day you happen to decide.

In Toyama

Winters here run on without the windows being opened.

So autumn feels like the window before that. Except that while you are still thinking so, it turns cold, and the screen goes into winter torn.

Seeing out the year behind a screen full of blossoms, I tell myself next year — every year.
