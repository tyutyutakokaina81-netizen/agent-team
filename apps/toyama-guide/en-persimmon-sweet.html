Two Kinds of the Same Fruit Look Identical

You cannot tell them apart by looking.

Persimmons come sweet and astringent. Both ripen to the same orange and hang from the branch the same way.

One bite settles it

Anyone who has bitten into an astringent one remembers the sensation.

Not sourness — something that pulls all the moisture out of your mouth at once. Closer to a texture than a taste, and it stays for a while after you swallow.

Do it once and you stop biting casually into unfamiliar persimmons.

I do not know how to tell

The variety decides it, I am told.

But I do not know anyone who can look at a tree and say that one is sweet. Households with a persimmon tree in the garden pick from the same tree every year, so the question never comes up.

You rarely meet a new tree in the first place.

The astringent ones are not wasted

They are not eaten as they are, but dried they turn sweet.

I have written about that elsewhere. The notable part is that simply leaving them alone changes them, which is not how other fruit behaves.

Sweet ones eaten as they are, astringent ones hung up to dry. The same coloured fruit, two destinations.

In Toyama

You still see persimmon trees at the edge of a garden or a field.

Often there is more fruit than anyone will pick, and it is simply left. The uncollected ones colour up, birds come, and eventually they fall. Whether that particular tree was sweet or astringent, nobody ever finds out.

Fact-check note
  • ✅ Persimmons are divided into sweet and astringent types; the astringency comes from tannins.
  • ✅ Astringent persimmons lose the astringency and become sweet when dried (among other treatments).
  • ⚠️ "You cannot tell by looking" is the writer's experience. If you know the variety, you can tell — the text says the variety decides it.
  • ⚠️ The description of astringency is the writer's wording, not a physiological account.
  • ⚠️ "I know nobody who can tell" and "more fruit than anyone picks" are what the writer sees, not statistics.
  • ⚠️ Methods of removing astringency other than drying are not covered.
  • ⚠️ This is not an encouragement to eat fruit from unknown trees.
  • ⚠️ No district or household is identified (prefecture level only).
