The Tree Whose Fruit Nobody Picks

One tree here carries fruit every autumn and nobody strips it: the pomegranate. It grows in gardens and inside the walls that run along the road, and when autumn comes the red fruit hangs there and stays.

They split, then they fall

Ripe ones open themselves. The skin cracks from the top until the red seeds inside are visible from the outside, and in that state they go on hanging for a while.

Rain gets in. Birds work at them. Eventually they drop, and at this time of year you see the fallen ones burst on the ground. Edible fruit, ending uneaten. It looks like a waste — though I have never gone and picked any either.

Nobody remembers how to eat them

Bite the seeds and there is a sharp sweetness. But the edible part is only the thin layer around each stone, and there is not much of it.

Whether you spit the stones out or crush and swallow them apparently depends on the household. I ate them as a child and cannot recall how we dealt with that. What I remember is that my hands went red.

Little to eat for a lot of work. That is probably why the fruit in gardens is left alone.

Barely on sale

In the shops I go to, fresh pomegranate rarely appears. It turns up more often as juice, or in some processed form.

I cannot remember it being treated as one of the autumn foods either, the way chestnuts and sweet potatoes are. It grows in the garden and yet has no real place at the table — a fruit with the wrong kind of presence.

In Toyama

You see the trees in gardens, more often at older houses. That is an impression rather than a count.

At this time of year I walk past a wall with red fruit showing over the top of it. I have walked past that tree every autumn, and every autumn I have done nothing about it.

Fact-check note
  • ✅ Pomegranates ripen in autumn and the skin splits open naturally, exposing the seeds.
  • ✅ The edible part is the juicy layer around each seed — little yield for the effort.
  • ✅ Pomegranate is planted as a garden tree in Japan.
  • ⚠️ "Rarely in the shops" refers to the shops the writer visits; this varies by area and store.
  • ⚠️ "More often at older houses" is an impression, not a count.
  • ⚠️ Whether to spit or swallow the stones is given as something that differs by household — no correct method is asserted.
  • ⚠️ No health or medicinal claims are made.
  • ⚠️ Why garden fruit goes unpicked is the writer's inference, not research.
