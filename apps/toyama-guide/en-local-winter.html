Travel · Toyama, Japan · Winter

A local's winter in Toyama

Summer here is generous, but ask a local and many will tell you winter is when Toyama really shows off. This is the everyday version — what a cold season tastes and feels like from someone who grew up in Takaoka, not a highlight reel. It's grey skies, deep snow, and a bay that turns out some of the best cold-weather food in Japan. If you're weighing when to come, this is the counterpart to a local's summer.

The bay's cold-season prize: yellowtail and crab

Winter is when Toyama Bay is at its richest. Locals wait all year for kanburi — cold-season yellowtail, fatty and rich, at its best in the depths of winter and often landed around Himi. It's eaten as sashimi, seared, or simmered in a hot pot (buri-shabu) that warms the whole table. Alongside it comes red snow crab (benizuwaigani), sweet and delicate — see the Toyama crab guide. Even a modest place serves fish in winter that would cost a fortune elsewhere. (Raw fish is best from a trusted counter; if in doubt, have it cooked.)

Snow country, up close

Toyama sits in one of Japan's snowiest regions. In the mountains the snow piles metres deep — it's the same snow that, come spring, becomes the famous snow corridor on the Alpine Route. Down in the towns it's less dramatic but ever-present: shovelling the drive, the hiss of tyres on slush, and the particular hush a street takes on under fresh snow. It isn't a postcard so much as a rhythm you live with.

The Alps over a winter sea

The single most striking sight of the season is the Tateyama range appearing to rise straight out of Toyama Bay. It happens on clear, cold mornings, and winter is when the air is sharpest and the peaks whitest — best caught from the Amaharashi coast or the fishing town of Himi. It's the kind of view that explains why people put up with the grey days in between.

Staying warm, the everyday way

Winter life indoors has its own small comforts: the kotatsu — a low table with a heater and a heavy quilt — that the whole family disappears under and refuses to leave; a bubbling pot of nabe for dinner; and, when you can get to one, a hot-spring soak with steam rising into cold air. None of it is grand. It's the quiet, warm heart of a snow-country winter.

When to come, and how

The cold-food season runs roughly November to February, with crab and yellowtail at their peak in the deep of winter. Snow can affect mountain access, so check conditions. Toyama is about 15 minutes from Kanazawa by shinkansen — an easy day trip from Kanazawa — though a night lets you pair a seafood dinner with a coast morning. A note on honesty: this is one resident's view, not a definitive account. Seasons, catches and conditions shift year to year, so treat it as a starting point rather than a checklist — and check current details before you travel.
