Travel · Toyama, Japan · Autumn

A local's autumn in Toyama

If you're deciding when to come to Japan for the leaves, here's a quiet argument for Toyama: some of the country's earliest autumn colour turns here, up in the mountains, while the rest of Japan is still green. This is the everyday version of the season from someone who grew up in Takaoka — not a highlight reel. It's the counterpart to a local's summer and a local's winter.

Japan's earliest foliage, up on the mountains

Most people plan a Japan leaf trip around late-November Kyoto. But high on the Tateyama Kurobe Alpine Route, the colour starts far earlier — foliage around the Murodo plateau is generally at its best from late September into early October, among the earliest in the country, then slides down the slopes over the following weeks. Because the route climbs nearly two kilometres in altitude, the season is unusually long: catch it high in late September, or lower down through October. If your dates are fixed early, this is a rare way to see real koyo weeks before the famous southern spots turn. (Mountain weather is fickle and the route runs on a seasonal calendar, so check conditions and closing dates before you go — see the Alpine Route by season.)

The gorge in colour

Lower down, autumn's other showpiece is the Kurobe Gorge, where a little open-sided torokko train winds along a river valley between steep, wooded walls. Through October and into November the slopes turn red and gold above the water. It's slower and less crowded than the marquee koyo spots — the kind of place you can still hear the river over the crowd.

New rice, and a bay changing gear

Autumn is harvest here. Toyama is a serious rice region, and the arrival of shinmai — the year's new rice — is a small event; a bowl of freshly harvested rice, glossy and faintly sweet, is genuinely a reason to eat well in autumn. The bay shifts too, moving out of summer and toward the cold-season fish that peak in winter. It's a between-season table: the last of the light summer food, the first of the richer autumn cooking, and rice at its best. For the wider picture, see the Toyama food guide.

The everyday feel of the season

Down in the towns, autumn is the easiest season to just be outside. The heavy summer humidity lifts, the light turns low and golden by late afternoon, and the evenings need a light jacket. Persimmons appear on garden trees and get hung to dry under the eaves. It's short — winter comes early to snow country — which is part of why locals make the most of the clear days while they last.

When to come, and how

Roughly speaking: aim for late September to early October for the high mountains, and mid-October into November for the gorge and the lowland colour. Autumn is one of Japan's two peak seasons, so mountain lodging and the Alpine Route book up early — plan ahead. Toyama is about 15 minutes from Kanazawa by shinkansen, an easy day trip from Kanazawa, and sits off the Golden Route — quieter than Kyoto in leaf season. A note on honesty: this is one resident's view, not a definitive account. Foliage timing shifts year to year — a hot summer tends to push the peak later — and mountain access depends on weather and the route's seasonal schedule. Treat this as a starting point, and check current forecasts and dates before you travel.
