You Notice the Ones That Left

The swap registers from the side that disappears.

Birds arriving and birds departing are trading places in autumn. What I notice always starts as come to think of it, I haven't seen those lately.

The eaves go quiet

Through spring and summer some houses have a nest under the eaves.

The traffic in and out is impossible to miss, and a few people fix a board underneath to catch the droppings. Then at some point it goes quiet.

I never know when the last one left. By the time I notice, the nest is already empty.

The arrivals are harder to see

I do see birds coming down onto the cut paddies.

But I cannot tell whether they have been here since summer or have just arrived. I do not know their names either — from a distance I think only there are birds.

Watching without knowing

Someone who knows would identify them at a glance, and looking it up would settle it.

I have not done that, and I go on watching the same scene every year without doing it. What I retain is only that there is a period when white shapes are dotted across the fields.

In Toyama

The plain holds a lot of rice fields, which is probably why birds settle on the cut ones so readily.

People say the closeness of mountains and sea makes this a route, though I could not tell you which birds pass where.

In winter there are sometimes only tracks left on the snow. I did not see the animal, but I know it came. That is about the distance at which we pass each other, year after year.
