One Whole, or Half

The decision happens in front of the shelf.

A whole napa cabbage, or half of one. Half will definitely get used — I know that, and I still think the whole one is better value.

The size comes first

It will not fit in the fridge.

So it lives in the corner of the kitchen under a sheet of newspaper. Keeping that way is supposed to be a point in its favour, except that it also means several days of knowing there is still cabbage.

The number of days it will take to finish is visible before I have even bought it.

The menu bends towards it

Hotpot, simmered, stir-fried, pickled.

The order is more or less fixed, and at the end it either gets salted down or disappears a little at a time into miso soup.

Every time I cut one open I have forgotten again that the outer leaves are tough and the middle is sweet.

People say the cold changes it

Frost improves it, is the usual way of putting it.

Eaten with that in mind it does seem true. But I have never compared the same plant before and after, and since I knew the saying first, that may be doing the work.

In Toyama

Winter is long here, so the hotpot season is long too.

Buy one whole in October and by February you are still buying them the same way. September is probably the last month I buy half.

Before long there will be nothing left to decide at the shelf.

Fact-check note
  • ✅ Napa cabbage is a heading leaf vegetable, commonly sold whole, halved or quartered.
  • ✅ It keeps without refrigeration in a cool place, and a whole head keeps longer than a cut one.
  • ⚠️ "Frost makes it sweeter" is a widely repeated saying; the writer states in the text that he has not compared it.
  • ⚠️ "Outer leaves tough, middle sweet" is a general tendency and varies by plant and season.
  • ⚠️ "October through February" is the writer's own habit, not a statistic.
  • ⚠️ Whether a whole head is actually better value depends on the shop and the season — the text treats it as a judgement, not a fact.
  • ⚠️ No production figures or growing regions for Toyama are claimed; none were checked.
  • ⚠️ No shop, district or household is identified (prefecture level only).
