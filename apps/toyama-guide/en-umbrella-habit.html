Four Umbrellas in the Hall, and Nobody Can Say Whose They Are

Four umbrellas stand in the hall. Nobody in the house can account for more than one or two of them. I bought one, at some point. Another belonged to somebody here. The rest are unexplained.

How they arrive

The logic is simple: being caught out in the rain is worse than owning too many, so you scatter them. One in the hall, one at the workplace, a folding one in your bag, one left at school for years.

Scattering them means losing track of how many you have. So on some wet afternoon you decide you have none with you, and buy another from a convenience store — and that one joins the hall.

Cheap transparent umbrellas pile up in Japanese households for what is probably this reason. They cost very little and are sold at every station and corner shop, which means they can be bought at exactly the moment you have concluded you have none.

The names come off

The longer one stands in the hall, the more vague its ownership becomes.

You take whichever is nearest the door on the way out. No one comes to reclaim it. It was never declared communal — it simply ended up that way.

A broken rib does not get one thrown out, either. With one strut bent it just stops being chosen on rainy days, and goes on standing there.

Carrying one you never open

Plenty of days I take an umbrella and never open it. Possibly most days — though I have never kept count, so that is an impression rather than a fact.

The extra thing in your hand is a nuisance, and every time there is a small argument for leaving it behind. I take it anyway. A day spent soaked stays with you much longer than a day spent carrying something you did not need.

Why the hall never empties

From the second half of September into October, the morning sky here stops being reliable. It can be clear at eight and raining by noon. There are words for why; the mechanism is beyond me, and the practical effect is only that I stop making the day's decisions early.

So the umbrellas do not get used up, and they do not get thrown away. Of the ones standing by the door, the ones I remember buying are probably outnumbered by the ones that simply turned up.
