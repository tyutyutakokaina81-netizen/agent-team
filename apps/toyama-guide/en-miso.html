I Never Actually Chose It

I do not deliberate in front of the shelf.

When I buy miso I reach for the same one. I have no memory of comparing the dozen or so varieties beside it.

Chosen without choosing

I could not tell you when it became that one.

It is probably not what my family used. More likely it is whatever I bought first after moving here, and it simply continued. Run out, buy the same, repeat for a few years — and it stops being a choice at all.

I cannot describe the difference

I know the categories exist: red or white, rice or barley or soybean.

Knowing that, I still could not say which one is in my kitchen without reading the tub. And I could not describe the taste difference to anyone. I eat it every day and have never compared.

The strength, though, is decided

I have no opinion about the type and a firm one about the dilution.

Too thin and it feels like nothing; too strong and I am thirsty afterwards. The band between those is fixed somewhere in me, precise enough that I notice when a bowl falls outside it.

In Toyama

Households here are said to put a lot into the soup.

Root vegetables, tofu, sometimes fish trimmings. Perhaps all that gives up enough of its own flavour that less miso is needed — and writing that, I realise I have never checked it either.

Standing at the miso shelf without hesitating is probably just never having felt the need to hesitate.
