The Knife Stops Partway

The blade goes in and then will not go further.

Trying to halve a kabocha, it jams about a third of the way through the skin. Push or pull and nothing moves; even getting it back out takes effort.

I do not know how hard to press

Should I lean my weight on it, or work the blade back and forth?

I have tried both and lost my nerve partway through both. Once you picture what happens if your hand slips, there is a limit to how hard you are willing to press. The problem is less that it is hard to cut than that I do not know how much force I am allowed to use.

Some people heat it first

Someone told me to microwave it briefly before cutting.

It does let the blade in. But overdo it and the cut face collapses, and once it cools it is as hard as before — so the right amount is different every time.

Why it is sold in halves

At the shop you see whole ones, halves and quarters side by side.

I had assumed the order of causation was: cutting is difficult at home, therefore they sell it pre-cut. Except that cut surfaces spoil faster. Each time, I am choosing again between the two.

In Toyama

The custom of eating kabocha at the winter solstice is spoken of here too.

Whether I actually keep a whole one around until December — I do not. I buy it when the day comes. Presumably people once stored them; I have never checked.

Each time the knife stops, I think for a second, and push again.
