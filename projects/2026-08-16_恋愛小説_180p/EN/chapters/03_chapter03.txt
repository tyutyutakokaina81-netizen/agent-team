CHAPTER THREE — The Night of the Firefly Squid

When April came, the air of the town softened, little by little.
The color of the sea shifted from the deep blue of winter to the pale blue of spring. The buds of the pines in the grove stretched into a bright green. And at night, from the direction of the fishing harbor, a liveliness that had not been there before began to reach her on the wind.
"It's hotaru-ika season," Matsuzo said.
Firefly squid. Haruka knew the name. But she had never seen the real thing. In a Tokyo supermarket she had seen them packed in trays — boiled, dressed with vinegar-miso, those tiny squid.
"Those are the corpses of firefly squid," Matsuzo said, and gave a snort. "The real thing glows. The whole night sea glows blue. You've never seen it, have you."
"No."
"Then go and see," Matsuzo said. "Tonight, there might be a beaching."
"A beaching?"
"The firefly squid come up onto the shore at night, to spawn," Minato explained from beside them. "On a calm night around the new moon. The whole beach glows blue. Even the locals don't get to see it often."
Haruka tried to picture it, and could not quite. The whole beach, glowing blue. It sounded like something not of this world.
"Shall we go and see it tonight?" Minato asked her.
Haruka looked at him, surprised. It was the first time Minato had invited her to anything himself.
"Is that all right?" she asked.
"Tonight's the new moon," Minato said. "No wind. If Matsuzo-san says there'll be a beaching, there probably will be. I want to take photographs, too."
That night, Haruka bundled up in warm clothes and set off for the shore.
At the appointed time, she met Minato in front of Migiwa. He was carrying a tripod and camera. The night beach was an entirely different place from the day one. There were no streetlights and no moon; the whole world was dark, and only the sound of the waves came out of the black.
"Watch your feet," Minato said, lighting the ground at her feet with a flashlight. "There are a lot of rocks along here."
The two of them walked the beach.
For a while, nothing happened. The sea was simply black, the waves lifting only their white edges into the dark before they vanished. Little by little, Haruka grew cold. She began to think the firefly squid would not come.
"They'll come," Minato said, as if reading her mind. "Let's wait just a little longer."
The two of them sat on a piece of driftwood and looked at the sea.
In the dark, Minato's profile was almost invisible. Only the fact of his presence, close beside her, was there. Had she ever, Haruka wondered, sat beside someone like this, silently gazing into the dark? In Tokyo, the gaps had always been filled — with words, with sound, with light. Silence was an awkward thing. But now, the silence with Minato was not awkward. If anything, it was comfortable.
"Do you always shoot alone?" Haruka asked.
"Yes," Minato said. "Every morning. The Tateyama across the sea, beyond Meiwa Rock. Alone, at an hour when no one comes."
"Every morning?"
"For two years now," Minato said. "And I still haven't taken one that I can call the one."
"Two years. The same place, for two years."
"Even the same place is never the same morning twice," Minato said. "The light, the clouds, the waves, how the mountains show — every morning is different. Someday the morning I want to photograph will come. I'm waiting for it."
"What kind of morning do you want to photograph?"
Minato was quiet for a while.
Then, slowly, he said, "Probably — the coldest morning of the year. A deep-winter morning, windless, clear. The air frozen, the sea gone perfectly still, and beyond Meiwa Rock, the whole Tateyama Range, sharp and complete. There are only a few such mornings in a year. I want to photograph the light of one of them."
Haruka looked at his profile in the dark.
"Ah," Minato said softly.
Haruka followed his gaze.
The sea was glowing.
At the water's edge, points of blue light had appeared. At first only a few. But when the next wave came in, the light increased. A pale, phantom-blue light, carried by the waves, washing in toward the shore, and in, and in.
"The beaching," Minato whispered.
Haruka stood. She ran to the water's edge.
There, countless firefly squid had been cast ashore. Each one gave off a blue light. It was as if the stars of the night sky had come down whole to the beach. Haruka crouched and lifted one gently onto her palm.
Cold, tiny — and yet, certainly, glowing.
It was the light of a life. A light of small creatures that had come to the shore at the cost of their lives, to spawn — their last light. Haruka gazed at it in her palm.
For some reason, tears rose.
This was a light for death. And yet, at the same time, it was a light to bring forth new life. Ending and beginning were in the same single light.
"It's beautiful," Haruka whispered.
The word was not enough. But there was no other word.
A little away, Minato had set up his tripod. The sound of the shutter rang quietly in the dark.
Haruka returned the firefly squid on her palm gently to the sea. The blue light was carried away by the waves and vanished into the dark.
"Yuzuki-san," she said, turning to him. "Did you get it? The photograph — the one?"
Minato was peering into the camera's monitor.
"No," he said, with a small laugh. "This isn't it either. But — it's not bad."
For a while, the two of them looked at the glowing sea.
Before long, the beaching subsided. The blue lights thinned, and when the last one was returned to the waves, the sea went back to being an ordinary black sea. As if what they had seen had been a dream.
On the way back, Haruka and Minato walked the dark beach side by side.
"Thank you for showing me," Haruka said. "I'd never seen anything like it."
"I see it every year," Minato said. "And every year I'm astonished, as if for the first time. I'm glad I could show you something good."
In front of Migiwa, they parted.
"Good night," Haruka said.
"Good night," Minato said. And then, as though hesitating, he added, "There'll be coffee in the morning, too."
Haruka smiled.
"I'll come."
Climbing the hill home, Haruka could not sleep for a while.
When she closed her eyes, that blue light floated up behind them. The single light of ending and beginning.
And, beside that light, Minato's profile.
Deep in her chest, Haruka felt something begin to glow, small, like a firefly squid.
She did not yet know the name of that light.
Or perhaps she knew it, and was pretending not to.
