> Toyama gets skipped a lot because people rush between Kanazawa and the Alpine Route, but it's a genuinely good detour if you have a spare day. Toyama Bay is one of Japan's richest fishing grounds, so even a modest sushi spot punches above its weight (white shrimp is the local thing). Takaoka, ~20 min from Toyama, is a 400-year metal-casting town — a lot of Japan's temple bells are cast there — and it's also the hometown of Doraemon's creator, with a small manga-pilgrimage angle. Himi is a working fishing port where, on clear winter days, you can see the Tateyama mountains rising straight out of the sea. It's quiet and un-touristy, which is the point.
>

## 予備の文（テーマ違いのスレ用・使い回し可）

**A. 金沢からの日帰りを聞かれている時** → リンク `en-daytrip.html`
> If you're based in Kanazawa, Takaoka + Himi make an easy half-day to full-day trip by local train (about 20 min to Takaoka). Takaoka is an old metal-casting town — the Great Buddha, a historic foundry district, and Doraemon touches around town — and Himi is a fishing port with Alps-over-the-sea views on clear winter days. Low-key and not crowded. Check current train times officially before you go.

**B. 立山黒部アルペンルートを聞かれている時** → リンク `en-alpine-season.html`
> The Alpine Route is seasonal — it opens in spring and closes in late autumn — and the famous snow corridor is tallest in the earlier weeks after opening, shrinking as summer comes. Exact dates are set each year and shift with the snow, so don't lock flights around a guess; confirm the current year's dates on the official site. If you want the season/snow-wall timing in plain English I can link a local rundown.
